### 5.2  Police Oversight & Body‑Camera Use  

**Story Highlights (Feb 14 2024):**  

- **26,000** service calls in 2023 → **6** complaints filed.  
- **5** complaints **exonerated**; **1** resulted in a **written reprimand**.  
- **Body‑camera activation** identified as a recurring gap – “lack of activation when officers interact with the public.”  
- **Charging Committee**: 5‑member panel reviews investigations, uses state disciplinary matrix.  
- **Trial Board**: Currently no cases referred; judge **Owen Wise** available for future trials.  

**Why It’s Critical:**  

- **Public trust** hinges on transparency.  
- **Policy gaps** (e.g., body‑camera activation) can be directly linked to **misconduct claims** and **civil‑rights lawsuits**.  

**Story Angles:**  

1. **Audit of Body‑Camera Policies:** Compare Talbot Police Department’s body‑camera SOPs with Caroline County’s identified gaps. Request the **latest PAB recommendations** and see if Talbot has adopted similar changes.  
2. **Community Perception Survey:** Conduct a short poll of Easton, Oxford, and St. Michaels residents on confidence in local policing. Use the PAB’s statistics as a benchmark.  
3. **Legal Implications:** Interview **Attorney General’s Office** on how the **Maryland Police Accountability Act of 2021** is being enforced statewide.  

**Potential Sources:**  

- **John Bartlett** (PAB Chair) – for current recommendations.  
- **Talbot County Police Public‑Affairs Officer** – for policy statements.  
- **Legal experts** (e.g., *University of Maryland School of Law* professor specializing in police reform).

### 5.3  Human Trafficking & Exploitation  

**Key Points (Jan 31 2025):**  

- **For All Seasons** reports **ongoing trafficking** in Talbot County.  
- **120** people completed the **free online anti‑trafficking course** (since September).  
- The organization operates as a **regional navigator** for Caroline, Dorchester, Kent, Queen Anne’s, and Talbot counties.  
- Council members **Keasha Haythe** and **Pete Lesher** publicly recognize the issue (proclamation for National Human‑Trafficking Awareness Month).  

**Public‑Health Intersection:**  

- Trafficking victims often suffer **physical injuries, mental‑health trauma, and infectious‑disease risks**.  
- Lack of reporting (“decline in self‑referrals”) suggests **under‑identification**.  

**Story Angles:**  

1. **Service‑Gap Mapping:** Identify **local service providers** (e.g., shelters, crisis hotlines) and assess capacity versus estimated victim count.  
2. **Law‑Enforcement Collaboration:** Examine how Talbot PD works with **For All Seasons** and the **state’s Human Trafficking Task Force**. Request recent arrest or prosecution data.  
3. **Community Awareness:** Evaluate the reach of the **online course**—who is taking it (law‑enforcement, social workers, general public)? Suggest expanding in‑person workshops at community centers (e.g., **Oxford Community Center**).  

**Potential Sources:**  

- **Lauren Weber** (VP Strategy) – for program data.  
- **Katie Theeke** – for outreach strategy.  
- **Talbot County Social Services Director** – for victim‑services capacity.

### 5.4  Alcohol Licensing & Public‑Safety  

**Key Facts (Jan 10 2025):**  

- **Application deadline:** March 31, 2025.  
- **Renewal rule:** Automatic approval unless **≥10** residents/property owners file a **protest** ≥30 days before expiration (April 30).  
- **License expiration:** April 30, 2024 → May 1 posting required.  
- **Contact:** **Victoria Bradley** (Secretary).  

**Why It’s Health‑Related:**  

- **Alcohol‑related crashes**, **DUIs**, and **public‑intoxication** directly affect emergency‑room admissions.  
- **License density** (number of bars, restaurants) correlates with **violent‑crime spikes** in many jurisdictions.  

**Story Angles:**  

1. **License‑to‑Risk Analysis:** Gather data on **DUI arrests** and **alcohol‑related ED visits** over the past three years; compare to the **number of active liquor licenses** in each municipality (Easton, Oxford, etc.).  
2. **Community Protest Review:** Identify any **protests filed** for the 2025 cycle (public records). Explore motivations (e.g., noise, safety, traffic).  
3. **Under‑age Drinking Prevention:** Interview school counselors (e.g., **Easton High School**) about **education programs** and any collaboration with the Liquor Board.  

**Potential Sources:**  

- **Talbot County Health Department** – for alcohol‑related health stats.  
- **Talbot County Police** – DUI enforcement data.  
- **Local business owners** – perspective on licensing impact.

### 5.5  Infrastructure & Safety Planning  

**Pedestrian‑Bridge Cost Study (Dec 5 2024):**  

- **Preliminary estimate:** **$8.1 M** for a bridge over MD 50 at Cracker Barrel location.  
- **Comparables:** Tysons Old Meadow Bridge ($8 M), Lane Place Bridge ($10.8‑$12.4 M).  
- **Funding:** $120 k from Baltimore Metropolitan Council (BMC) for feasibility study; potential **state/federal grant** involvement.  

**Safety Implications:**  

- Bridges improve **walkability**, encourage **active transportation**, and can **reduce traffic‑related injuries**.  
- Elevated design (higher than vehicle overpasses) may raise **construction‑safety concerns** for workers and the public.  

**Story Angles:**  

1. **Cost‑Benefit Review:** Interview **Shane Moore** (Chief Roads Engineer) and **local advocacy groups** about projected **pedestrian‑safety improvements** vs. cost.  
2. **Community Input:** Host a town‑hall (virtual or at **Talbot County Senior Center**) to gather resident concerns about bridge location, aesthetics, and accessibility (ADA compliance).  

**Potential Sources:**  

- **Shane Moore** – Chief Roads Engineer.  
- **Kimley‑Horn** (consulting firm) – for design rationale.  
- **Talbot County Planning Department** – for long‑term mobility plans.

## 8️⃣  Reporting Checklist (Bullet‑Point “How‑to”)  

- **Before the Interview:**  
  - Verify the interviewee’s title and contact details (use the People Tracker).  
  - Review relevant **public records** (court filings, licensing applications, PAB minutes).  
  - Draft a **list of open‑ended questions** that tie personal experience to broader trends.  

- **During the Interview:**  
  - Ask for **specific examples** (e.g., “Can you describe the last time a body‑camera was not activated?”).  
  - Request **quantitative details** (exact numbers, dates, budget figures).  
  - Clarify any **legal terminology** (e.g., “written reprimand” vs. “suspension”).  

- **After the Interview:**  
  - Fact‑check every statistic against **official data sources** (state open data portals, agency reports).  
  - Seek **independent verification** (e.g., a second law‑enforcement official, a public‑health nurse).  
  - If the story involves an **ongoing investigation**, add a **caveat** (see Section 9).

## 10️⃣  Practical Tips for the Talbot Health & Public‑Safety Beat  

1. **Build a “Hot‑Sheet” of Local Data Portals** – Maryland Open Data, Talbot County GIS, State Health Department dashboards.  
2. **Attend County Board Meetings** (Council, Liquor Board, Police Oversight) – minutes often contain leads (e.g., upcoming license protests, budget allocations for policing).  
3. **Leverage Regional Partnerships** – For All Seasons, Maryland Department of Public Safety, neighboring county law‑enforcement agencies.  
4. **Use FOIA Requests Wisely** – Request recent **complaint logs** from TCPD, **DUI arrest statistics**, or **human‑trafficking case files** (redacted where required).  
5. **Stay Alert to Seasonal Risks** – Summer boating accidents, winter storms affecting flood‑risk (e.g., Federalsburg Flood exhibit) – tie environmental events to health outcomes.  
6. **Maintain a “Story Calendar”** – Map out recurring events (e.g., National Human‑Trafficking Awareness Month in January, licensing deadline March 31) to plan ahead.
