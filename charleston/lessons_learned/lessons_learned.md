3. What specific advice would you give to fellow journalism students about using LLMs to understand news stories or archives?
Every and any result produced by an LLM should be taken with a grain of salt.

We are at an age when many LLM results still need to be fact-checked, and, for the sake of saving time, it's unnecessary to continue using something that needs to be verified rather than conducting the research yourself. On the other hand, I understand the need for efficiency, especially in journalism, and even the most basic tasks could be trusted to an LLM, but even then, double-check. 

Also, LLMs are sponges that can easily be tainted. The information you provide for context will determine the perspective and mindset it adopts until a new chat, sometimes a new account, is used. Be careful with opinions, statements, and quotations when feeding articles to an LLM. Providing articles from a conservative news outlet or a conservative perspective might instill the same mindset and perspective in the LLM.
