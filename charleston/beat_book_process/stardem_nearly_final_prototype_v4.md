### Slide 2 – Beat Scope & Why It Matters  
- **Geographic focus:** All of Talbot County *plus* its five incorporated towns.  
- **Core topics:**  
  1. **Substance regulation & licensing** – cannabis, alcohol, tobacco.  
  2. **Crime & policing** – police oversight, human‑trafficking, violent incidents.  
  3. **Infrastructure & environmental health** – water & sewer fees, bridge safety, flood‑risk.  
- **Public‑interest angle:** Each issue touches everyday life – what you drink, how safe you feel walking downtown, whether a neighbor is being exploited.

### Slide 3 – The Physical Landscape  
- **Key natural features:** Chesapeake Bay (eastern edge), Miles River, Eastern Bay, Choptank River, Marshyhope Creek, Tilghman Island.  
- **Historic sites that shape community identity:** St. Michael’s Maritime Museum, Talbot County Historical Society, Talbot Historical Society (Easton).  
- **Why geography matters:** Proximity to water drives both **public‑health risks** (flooding, water‑borne disease) and **law‑enforcement challenges** (maritime smuggling, trafficking routes).

### Slide 5 – Theme 1: Substance Regulation & Licensing  

**Key Storylines**  

- **Cannabis Ordinance – Easton (2024)**  
  - *What happened:* Ordinance 809 permits dispensaries in central‑business & general‑commercial zones; bans on‑site consumption.  
  - *Why it matters:* First municipal framework after Maryland’s 2023 recreational‑cannabis law; raises questions about zoning, odor‑control, public‑health impact.  
  - *Key institutions:* Easton Town Council, Planning & Zoning Dept. (Miguel Salinas), Maryland House Bill 805 (state‑level).  
  - *People to watch:* Council President **Frank Gunsallus**, Councilor **Don Abbatiello**, Town Planner **Lynn Thomas**, Town Attorney **Sharon VanEmburgh**.  

- **Liquor‑Board Licensing – Talbot County (2025)**  
  - *What happened:* New online application portal; deadline Mar 31 2025; renewal process can be challenged by 10 resident protests.  
  - *Why it matters:* Controls number of alcohol‑serving venues, ties to public‑safety (e.g., DUI rates, under‑age drinking).  
  - *Key institutions:* Talbot County Liquor Board, County Code Article 2, Secretary **Victoria Bradley**.  

**Potential Angles**  

- **“First‑Year Impact”** – Compare sales, complaints, and DUI statistics before/after the cannabis ordinance.  
- **“Community Voice”** – Interview residents who filed protests on liquor‑license renewals; explore “NIMBY” vs. “economic‑development” arguments.  
- **“Health‑Safety Overlap”** – Look at emergency‑room visits for cannabis‑related incidents; cross‑reference with liquor‑board data on bar‑related injuries.  

*Visual:* Side‑by‑side infographic (cannabis dispensary icon vs. liquor‑license form) with key dates and a quick “stats at a glance” box.

### Slide 6 – Theme 2: Crime & Policing Oversight  

**Key Storylines**  

- **Police Accountability Board (Caroline County) – 2023‑2024**  
  - *What happened:* 2,600+ calls for service; only six complaints; one resulted in a written reprimand.  
  - *Why it matters for Talbot:* Demonstrates regional standards for police transparency; Talbot may adopt similar oversight mechanisms.  
  - *Institutions:* Caroline County Police Accountability Board, Administrative Charging Committee, Trial Board (Judge Owen Wise).  
  - *People:* Board Chair **John Bartlett**, Administrator **Kimberly Raeder**, Deputy Chief **Rodney Helmer** (Sheriff’s Office).  

- **Human‑Trafficking Awareness – For All Seasons (Talbot) – 2025**  
  - *What happened:* 120+ people completed free anti‑trafficking online course; increased community reporting.  
  - *Why it matters:* Talbot’s location on the Bay makes it a potential transit point; “hidden” victims need services.  
  - *Institutions:* Non‑profit **For All Seasons**, Talbot County Council (Vice President **Pete Lesher**), local law‑enforcement liaison.  
  - *People:* VP **Lauren Weber**, VP‑Marketing **Katie Theeke**, Council Member **Keasha Haythe**.  

**Potential Angles**  

- **“From Calls to Complaints”** – Investigate why low complaint rates exist despite high call volumes; interview community members about trust in police.  
- **“Trafficking in Plain Sight”** – Map known trafficking routes; profile a survivor (with consent) to illustrate hidden dangers.  
- **“Oversight Transfer”** – Assess whether Talbot could create its own Police Accountability Board; compare funding models.  

*Visual:* Flowchart showing the three‑board structure (Accountability Board → Administrative Charging Committee → Trial Board) with a call‑out box for the single reprimand case.

### Slide 7 – Theme 3: Infrastructure & Environmental Health  

**Key Storylines (regional, useful for comparison)**  

- **Trappe Water‑Sewer Fee Ordinance (2024)** – Shows how municipalities raise fees to fund pumping‑station repairs; parallels Talbot’s aging septic systems.  
- **Pedestrian‑Bridge Cost Estimate (2024)** – $8 M projected for MD‑Route 50 crossing; underscores funding challenges for safe walkways.  

**Why It’s Relevant**  

- Talbot’s **Talbot County Hospital** and **Talbot County Health Department** rely on safe water and transport routes for emergency response.  
- Flood‑risk events (e.g., historic floods in Federalsburg) raise public‑health concerns (contaminated water, mold).  

**Potential Angles**  

- **“Hidden Costs of Clean Water”** – Survey Talbot homeowners on water‑bill trends; request county budget on pipe replacement.  
- **“Bridge Safety for Pedestrians”** – Interview MDOT on timelines; gather community feedback on crossing safety near the Miles River.  

*Visual:* Timeline graphic of major infrastructure projects (water‑sewer upgrades, bridge study) with budget amounts and projected completion dates.

### Slide 15 – Closing & Next Steps  

1. **Build a “beat calendar”** – mark all upcoming council meetings, board hearings, and community forums (e.g., Talbot County Council on May 19 2024).  
2. **Set up a source list** – maintain a spreadsheet with contacts, best times to reach them, and preferred communication method.  
3. **Identify a pilot story** – start with the *cannabis ordinance impact* because data is already public and the story is timely.  
4. **Schedule a briefing** with the **Talbot County Health Department** to get the latest health‑statistics and discuss possible collaborations on public‑safety reporting.  

> **Remember:** Every health‑or‑safety story in Talbot County ties back to the people who live, work, and commute there. Keep the community’s voice front‑and‑center, verify every statistic, and flag any unresolved issues with a clear “caveat” note.
